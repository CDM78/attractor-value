OBSERVATIONS_A: ADDED RISK FACTORS
1. ESG risk factor added: "Various stakeholders' increasing and changing expectations and new regulations with respect to Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) matters may impose additional costs on us or expose us to additional risks." This is an entirely new risk factor covering customer sustainability expectations, regulatory ESG requirements, and the potential for losing customers to more sustainable suppliers.

OBSERVATIONS_B: REMOVED RISK FACTORS
1. Brexit risk factor removed: "Brexit may adversely impact the Company's revenue and profits in the short term and long term." The standalone Brexit risk factor was eliminated; some Brexit-related language was folded into the tariffs/trade barriers risk factor as a brief example.

OBSERVATIONS_C: CHANGED SEVERITY
1. COVID-19 risk factor title changed from "could adversely affect" to "has significantly impacted" — shifting from hypothetical to acknowledged impact. The prior year detailed specific facility shutdowns with dates (Guangzhou Dec 2020, Chinese New Year 2020, India 2020); the current year generalizes these as "in late 2020, local governments... shut down certain of our facilities." The current year adds new language about difficulty hiring production workers during the pandemic and notes COVID outbreaks in 2021 causing production delays. The prior year discussed vaccination program uncertainty; the current year removes this and instead adds language that COVID "could exacerbate the other risk factors that we identify in this report."

2. Supply chain risk factor: Prior year title was "A disruption in our supply chain could adversely affect our profitability." Current year is substantively the same but adds new language about specific agriculture-related risks ("reduced crop yields, reduced crop availability, water shortages, increased water costs, reduced access to water, droughts, and other potentially more severe weather events"). Also adds a specific reference to 2021 onion harvest being "adversely impacted by both drought and flooding." The prior year had a specific reference to Chinese regulators shutting down synthetic color suppliers "within the last 36 months" — the current year generalizes this to "We have, in the past, dealt with regulators shutting down suppliers."

3. Cost/price volatility risk factor: Prior year title was "Commodity, energy, and transportation price volatility may reduce our profitability." Current year expands to "Raw material, energy, labor, and transportation cost volatility, including inflation in prices due to ongoing supply chain challenges and other macroeconomic forces, may reduce our profitability." New language explicitly references ongoing domestic and global supply chain issues, inflationary conditions, and the risk of losing customers if price increases are passed through.

4. Manufacturing operations risk factor: Current year adds "labor shortages," "ERP software issues" to the list of manufacturing risks, compared to prior year which did not include these.

5. World events/natural disasters risk factor: Current year adds substantial new language about dependence on favorable climatic conditions, specifically mentioning California drought and water supply issues affecting the Natural Ingredients business, and the 2021 onion harvest impacts.

6. Foreign operations risk factor: Title expands from "economic, political, and international operation risks" to "economic, political, security, and international operation risks" — adding "security" and adding "physical security risks" in the body text.

7. Tariffs risk factor: Current year absorbs Brexit-related trade risk as an example and adds new language about beneficial tariffs on U.S.-grown garlic and onion that could be reduced or eliminated.

8. Customer financial condition risk factor: Prior year referenced "some of the Company's customers entering bankruptcy or receivership during the past 24 months" and risk of others "in the next 12 months." Current year generalizes to "Historically, these combined pressures have resulted in some of the Company's customers entering bankruptcy" and "in the near-term." Current year also adds new language about pre-bankruptcy payments being subject to clawback.

9. LIBOR risk factor: Title changed from "Changes to LIBOR may negatively impact us" to "The transition away from LIBOR could negatively impact our borrowing costs." Updated to reflect that LIBOR cessation dates are now set (rather than expected), that the hardwired approach was adopted (rather than "will likely" be adopted), and that LIBOR-referencing debt decreased from ~20% to ~18% of total debt.

10. Divestitures/acquisitions risk factor: Prior year had two separate risk factors — one for divestitures and one for acquisitions. Current year consolidates these into a single "strategic transactions" risk factor. The divestiture language updates from pending fragrances sale to completed fragrances divestiture. The acquisition language adds new text about analyzing/evaluating acquisition opportunities and notes that acquisitions "may involve significant cash expenditures, debt incurrences, equity issuances, operating losses, and expenses." Environmental remediation estimate at Granada, Spain changed from $0.8 million to $0.9 million.

11. Consumer preferences risk factor: Prior year said "In the past year, we have seen a reduction in new product launches, smaller new product launches, and fewer limited time offerings." Current year changes to "Since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic, we have seen a reduction in the size of new product launches and fewer limited time offerings" — removing the reference to a reduction in the number of launches.

UNCERTAINTY_ASSESSMENT: CAN_ASSESS
The changes between years are clear and substantive. Multiple risk factors show meaningful updates reflecting realized operational challenges (supply chain disruption, inflation, labor shortages, climate/drought impacts) and new emerging risks (ESG). The removal of Brexit as a standalone risk and absorption into tariffs reflects resolution/normalization. Updates to LIBOR and divestitures reflect operational progress. However, several new risks have escalated in severity.

SCORE: 2.5

JUSTIFICATION: The risk profile shows a modest net deterioration. While some risks resolved or progressed (Brexit consolidated, LIBOR transition advancing, divestitures completed, LIBOR debt exposure slightly reduced from 20% to 18%), several factors point to worsening conditions: (1) the COVID risk factor shifted from hypothetical to realized impact language with new hiring difficulties noted; (2) supply chain and cost inflation risks were materially expanded with new specific language about ongoing domestic and global supply chain issues and explicit inflationary concerns; (3) climate/weather risks were substantially expanded with specific California drought and water supply concerns; (4) a new ESG risk factor was added reflecting growing stakeholder and regulatory pressure; (5) the manufacturing risk factor added labor shortages and ERP software issues. The net direction is toward a moderately more challenging risk environment, with cost/inflation and supply chain pressures being the most notable escalations.
