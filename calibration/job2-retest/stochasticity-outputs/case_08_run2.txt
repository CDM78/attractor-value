OBSERVATIONS_C: Several risk factors had notable language shifts:
1. Commodity risk heading softened from "Many of the products we manufacture or purchase and resell are commodities" to "A portion of the products we manufacture or purchase and resell are commodities." The current year also added "the impact of COVID-19 on residential construction, transportation constraints or disruptions" as new price volatility factors. The prior year's sentence about competitive LVL capacity ("New LVL capacity in the southeastern United States came online in late 2019, which we expect will add competitive volume and pricing pressure to the LVL marketplace") was removed.
2. COVID-19 risk factor shifted from "The full effect of the COVID-19 pandemic on our business is currently unknown" (prior) to "The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic...continue to evolve" (current), suggesting the risk is no longer framed as entirely unknown. However, the current year added a new sub-risk regarding "potential new regulations concerning mandatory COVID-19 vaccination" and the possibility that vaccine mandates "may have the near-term effect of increasing costs, straining company resources, interrupting operations, reducing employee morale, or increasing employee turnover." The prior year's extensive discussion of DHS essential infrastructure designation and state/local restrictions on construction was removed.
3. Manufacturing disruption risk added "which may increase in frequency, severity and duration due to the physical impacts of climate change" to the catastrophe bullet point, escalating severity of this sub-risk.
4. Labor risk removed pandemic-specific recruiting language ("those unemployed may not reside in the same areas as our manufacturing facilities, or may be unwilling to accept shift work or the physical nature of our positions") and replaced it with "changing workforce expectations including flexible or remote work arrangements that we may be unable to provide."
5. Wood fiber risk added "changing temperature and precipitation patterns" to the list of factors that may limit log availability, a new climate-related concern.
6. Stock price fluctuation risk added "investors' perception of our commitment to sustainability and corporate responsibility" as a new driver of stock volatility.
7. Environmental risk: Prior year included "including those aimed at addressing greenhouse gas emissions and climate change" in the general environmental risk factor. Current year removed this phrase from the general environmental section and elevated climate change into its own dedicated risk factor (see OBSERVATIONS_A item 2).
8. The current year reorganized all risk factors into sub-categories (Competitive Risks, Operational Risks, Financial Risks, Risks Relating to Laws and Regulations) whereas the prior year used only two broad categories (Risks Relating to Our Business, Risks Relating to Ownership of Our Common Stock).

JUSTIFICATION: The current year adds three new risk factors (data privacy, climate change, tax reform) and introduces climate-related language into several existing risks, which superficially suggests a more cautious posture. However, these additions are largely regulatory-awareness boilerplate reflecting broad industry and legal trends rather than company-specific deterioration. The COVID-19 risk framing shifted from "unknown" to "ongoing/evolving," suggesting modest normalization, and the LVL competitive capacity warning was removed. The commodity risk heading was softened from "many" to "a portion." The reorganization into sub-categories and addition of ESG/climate language appears to reflect enhanced disclosure practices rather than a materially changed risk profile. On balance, the changes are approximately offsetting — new disclosure risks are generic while some prior acute risks were moderated — resulting in a neutral assessment.
